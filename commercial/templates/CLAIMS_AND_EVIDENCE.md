# Public claims and evidence release checklist

**Goal:** give buyers confidence through verifiable proof, not superficial seals or fictional results.

Before publishing a statement, record:

| Claim | Product/version | Evidence source/date | Reviewer | Disclosure decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Exactly what is asserted] | [SKU/version] | [redacted test report, customer consent] | [reviewer] | HOLD until verified |

## Prohibited without authentic evidence
- "SOC 2 certified", "ISO 27001 certified", "pentested" or other certification or compliance badges.
- "99.99% uptime", "zero data loss", "100% secure", "completely autonomous" or guaranteed AI outcomes.
- Invented user counts, customer logos, testimonials, conversion improvements and transaction volume.
- Images or animation of simulated interfaces labeled as real product screenshots.
- Privacy/AI training policy that differs from actual providers and settings.
- Fictitious legal registration, address, full support team or on-call coverage.

## Required presentation components for each approved listing
1. Product version and factual one-sentence value proposition.
2. Feature coverage: works / partial / planned / unsupported.
3. Real annotated screenshots and authorized demo (synthetic data).
4. Source/implementation evidence or verified walk-through where permissible.
5. Prices, limits, recurring fees, taxes, usage billing and refund rules.
6. Clear AI/agent permissions, model/provider dependencies, human approval and safety limits.
7. Legal seller, contact, support hours, privacy notice and terms.
8. FAQ with realistic failure modes and scope exclusions.
9. Release/change history, known issues and dated test report.
10. Accessible disclosures, correctly linked and translated; no deceptive countdowns, fake scarcity or badges.

## Required gate
Two-person review is preferable where feasible; as a solo company, record an explicit owner sign-off plus dated technical evidence. For regulated or specialized claims, use an independent qualified reviewer.

An approved listing is **not** authorization for paid activation if payment delivery or the product's security/privacy gates remain unresolved.
